  I-groupoids with generators e ∈ E. Moreover, such an I-groupoid can be chosen to be compatible

  sense of Definition 2.17; compatible: Definition 2.19.) Its proof: "Start from an arbitrary
  finite I-groupoid G₀, or with G₀ := cym(H) in order to enforce compatibility with a given
  I-graph H. Then inductively apply Lemma 2.21 and note that the assumptions of the lemma are
  trivial for k = 1, because the trivial sub-groupoid generated by ∅, which just consists of the
  isolated neutral elements 1_s, is compatible with any I-graph. In each step as stated in the
  lemma, compatibility with corresponding chains implies that G* cannot have coset cycles of
  length up to N with cosets generated by sets of the form αβ_i were |α| ≤ 2k. For 2k = |E|, this
  rules out all coset cycles of length up to N." Lemma 2.21 (`amalgamationlem`) produces from G a
  finite G* with (i) G*_α ≅ G_α for |α| < 2k and (ii) compatibility with chains of G*_{αβ_i} for
  |α| ≤ 2k, by G* := cym(G ∪ H) for H a disjoint union of completed amalgamation chains; its
  proof obtains (i) from "The α'-reducts of α'-connected components of its completion arise as
  substructures of the completions of merged chains of components of the form G_{α'αβ},
  according to Lemma 2.9" — Lemma 2.9 (`completionandsubgroupslem`) being the statement that for
  an I-graph H and its α-reduct K = H↾α, "H̄↾α is an induced subgraph of K̄↾α … and the vertex
  set of H̄ is closed under α-edges within K̄" (completion commuting, up to induced substructure,
  with restriction to a sub-generator set).
