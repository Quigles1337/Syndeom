# A bounded monotone sequence of real numbers converges

**Theorem.** Let $(a_n)$ be a monotone (non-decreasing) sequence of real numbers that is bounded above. Then $(a_n)$ converges: there is $L \in \mathbb{R}$ with $a_n \to L$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1.** Lemma 1. A monotone sequence of real numbers that does not converge is not bounded above. Proof of the lemma: if such a sequence were bounded above, it would converge by the theorem, contrary to the assumption.

**Step 2.** Suppose, for contradiction, that $(a_n)$ does not converge. By Lemma 1 it is not bounded above, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $(a_n)$ converges.

∎
