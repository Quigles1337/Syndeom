# A polynomial identity in two integer variables

**Theorem.** For all integers $x$ and $y$, $(x - 2y)^2 = x^2 - 4yx + 4y^2$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1.** Expanding the square and factoring out $x$, $(x - 2y)^2 = -4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y)$.

**Step 2.** Rearranging the terms, $-4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y) = -4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x$.

**Step 3.** Rearranging the terms, $-4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x = x^2 - 4yx - 4 \cdot y \cdot y$.

**Step 4.** Rearranging the terms, $x^2 - 4yx - 4 \cdot y \cdot y = x^2 - 4yx - 4y^2$.

∎
