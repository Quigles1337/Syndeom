# A polynomial identity in two integer variables

**Theorem.** For all integers $x$ and $y$, $(x - 2y)^2 = x^2 - 4yx + 4y^2$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1.** Expanding the square and factoring out $x$, $(x - 2y)^2 = 4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y)$.

**Step 2.** Note the identity: for all integers $x$ and $y$, $(x + y)(x - y) = -y^2 + x^2$; both sides expand to the same polynomial.

**Step 3.** Rearranging the terms, $4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y) = 4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x$.

**Step 4.** As a check, the same polynomial admits another presentation: for all integers $x$ and $y$, $x(-4y + x) + 4y^2 = 4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y)$; both sides expand to the same polynomial.

**Step 5.** Rearranging the terms, $4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x = x^2 - 4yx + 4 \cdot y \cdot y$.

**Step 6.** Rearranging the terms, $x^2 - 4yx + 4 \cdot y \cdot y = x^2 - 4yx + 4y^2$.

∎
