# A polynomial identity in two integer variables

**Theorem.** For all integers $x$ and $y$, $(x - 2y)^2 = x^2 - 4yx + 4y^2$.

**Proof.**

**Step 1.** Lemma 1. For all integers $x$ and $y$, $4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y) = 4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x$; both sides expand to the same polynomial.

**Step 2.** Expanding the square and factoring out $x$, $(x - 2y)^2 = 4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y)$.

**Step 3.** By Lemma 1, $4 \cdot y \cdot y + x(x - 4y) = 4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x$.

**Step 4.** Rearranging the terms, $4y^2 - 4yx + x \cdot x = x^2 - 4yx + 4 \cdot y \cdot y$.

**Step 5.** Rearranging the terms, $x^2 - 4yx + 4 \cdot y \cdot y = x^2 - 4yx + 4y^2$.

∎
