No source found in this sweep falsifies CLAIM-0006 as stated: no paper found reports how well a proof-triage method detects errors broken down both by flaw class and by proof length for AI-generated research-level proofs. Several sources do narrow it, and they must be cited. They report detection by error type only, or by length or position only. Where they report both, the setting is competition-level, a single error type, or formal propositional logic.

## TL;DR

- **CLAIM-0006 survives, but only in a narrower form.** The closest prior work reports one of the two axes. PRMBench, AdvancedMathBench and IMProofBench break results down by error type. ProofGrid (formal propositional logic) and Li et al. 2024 (GSM8K-level, calculation errors over 2–9 steps) break them down by length or step count. None crosses flaw class with proof length on research-level, AI-generated proofs, and none defines a "fooling length".
- **Research-level verification benchmarks now exist.** SYNDEOM must position itself against them: Hard2Verify (ACL 2026), AdvancedMathBench/VerifierBench (arXiv 2607.11849), Open Proof Corpus, IMProofBench, First Proof (batches 1–2) and ArxivMathGradingBench (arXiv 2605.20531). Their verifier accuracies (for example, VerifierBench's best Balanced F1 of 65.1) are aggregate figures, not calibration curves.
- **Events in October 2026 make the problem concrete.** The openai/math release (722 manuscripts at the initial commit; three withdrawn and fourteen revised on 7 October — content S5 only), the AGMAI 29 September recommendations (comparator challenge file plus formalization.yaml) and Lean-hygiene audits ("Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking", leanprover/comparator) provide natural ground-truth anchors and a statement-fidelity and axiom-audit standard. SYNDEOM's detectors should be built on these.

## Key Findings

1. **The crux combination (flaw class × proof length, at research level) was not found anywhere.** The dedicated subagent pass confirmed that none of AdvancedMathBench, Hard2Verify, Open Proof Corpus, IMProofBench or PRMBench reports detector accuracy by proof length. PRMBench reports accuracy by error *position* (step index), which is a different variable.
2. **The nearest "type × length" result is elementary.** Li et al. (arXiv 2406.00755) built calculation-error solutions of 2 to 9 steps and report that identification accuracy "is not significantly affected by the number of steps". That is one error type at grade-school/MATH level. It narrows the claim but does not falsify it.
3. **The nearest "accuracy vs proof length" curve is in formal logic.** ProofGrid (Arkoudas & Batzoglou, arXiv 2605.12524) plots proof-checking accuracy "as a function of proof length". The proofs are propositional-logic proofs, not research mathematics, and its error "types" are verdict-error types (false accept, false reject, mislocalisation), not flaw classes.
4. **Synthetic flaw injection has well-established precedent.** PRMBench (GPT-4o rewrites PRM800K solutions into 9 error classes), FormalRewardBench (Lean, including a "Verbose Incorrect Proofs" strategy aimed squarely at length), FaithformBench (step perturbation), a Lean proof-repair mutation dataset (stratified by proof length) and BrokenMath (perturbed statements) all do this. SYNDEOM's generator is therefore methodologically precedented. Its novelty claim should rest on *dialable severity + length + 14-class labels + Lean/informal pairing*, not on injection itself.
5. **Statement fidelity is a recognised open problem with measured gaps.** Work in this area includes FormalAlign, "Beyond Compilation" (the compile–faithfulness gap across 8 systems), ShadowBench (SA-Pass, 178 research-level problems), RLMEval, miniF2F-Lean Revisited and "Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking". None reports fidelity-detector AUC as a function of statement length or complexity.
6. **Human-referee calibration data at research level is thin.** The evidence is mainly qualitative: Greiffenhagen 2024, Andersen, and Mejia-Ramos & Weber 2014. No blinded planted-error study of professional mathematicians was found. This is the area where SYNDEOM's human protocol is most novel and least anchored.

## E. Calibration methodology precedents

**Length-dependent detector performance (AI-text detection):**
- Sadasivan et al. "Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?" arXiv:2303.11156. Bounds the AUROC of *any* detector by the total-variation distance between the distributions, with TV analysed per sequence length. *Relation:* the **theoretical template for a "fooling length"**: detection falls to chance as distributions converge.
- Chakraborty, Bedi, Zhu, An, Manocha, Huang, "On the Possibilities of AI-Generated Text Detection" (arXiv:2304.04736v3; confirmed on arXiv). Its abstract states that "as machine-generated text approximates human-like quality, the sample size needed for detection increases". An aggregator summary reports ROC-AUC rising from about 0.6 for short texts to above 0.9 for paragraphs. **Those numbers are not in the abstract and remain UNCONFIRMED until the paper is read.**
- Ippolito et al. 2020, "Automatic Detection of Generated Text is Easiest when Humans are Fooled" (ACL 2020; arXiv:1911.00650; **verify**). Detector and human accuracy vary with excerpt length and decoding strategy.
- NBER Working Paper w34223, "Artificial Writing and Automated Detection." https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34223/w34223.pdf. AUROC by genre and length (short/medium/long passages) for commercial detectors.
- "Adversarial Creation and Detection of AI-Generated Social Bot Content." arXiv:2606.07219. AUC improves about 7.5% as text grows from 50 to more than 150 characters.
- "Detecting the Corruption of Online Questionnaires by AI." arXiv:2308.07499. Detectors at chance (AUC 0.446) on short texts. **An empirical "fell to chance" example**, though here it is short length, not long length, that defeats the detector.

*Interpretation:* in AI-text detection, *longer* inputs help the detector. SYNDEOM hypothesises the opposite for proof triage: longer proofs hide flaws. That direction reversal is itself a contribution worth stating, and it needs its own evidence.

**Synthetic flaw injection / mutation precedents** (from the subagent pass):
- PRMBench (A3). LLM-injected errors in 9 classes; 10% human audit (92% judged correct).
- **FormalRewardBench: A Benchmark for Formal Theorem Proving Reward Models.** arXiv:2605.10141. Lean; five strategies, including "Minimal Single-Point Variations" and "**Verbose Incorrect Proofs** … long proofs with many proof steps … test whether reward models can track correctness over extended reasoning". **The most length-relevant injection design found; must-cite.**
- "Learning to Repair Lean Proofs from Compiler Feedback." arXiv:2602.02990. Four mutation types (Theorem/Tactic/Line/Multi-line); splits stratified by proof length; per-type accuracy for *repair*.
- **BrokenMath: A Benchmark for Sycophancy in Theorem Proving with LLMs.** arXiv:2510.04721 (NeurIPS 2025). Perturbed *false statements* refined by experts (504 items). This is a precedent for SYNDEOM's "false theorem with convincing proof" class.
- **FaithformBench.** arXiv:2608.10916. 12,784 original/perturbed step pairs.
- Robustness of proof autoformalization (B8) and agentic review injection (D5).
- Authors of arXiv:2605.10141, 2602.02990, 2608.10916 and 2606.19749 were **not retrieved; verify.**

**Not found:** an explicit published "fooling length" or "break-even length" concept for proof checkers. Mutation testing for proof assistants and bug-injection benchmarks for code reviewers were **not searched** in this sweep. Plan a follow-up search covering classic mutation-testing and code-review bug-injection literature.

## F. Human referee calibration and cost

- Greiffenhagen, C. "Checking correctness in mathematical peer review." *Social Studies of Science* (2024). doi:10.1177/03063127231200274. Interview-based. Referees often don't check every detail and may weigh author reputation; one interviewee said refereeing is "almost as hard as writing your own paper if you do it well". *Relation:* qualitative grounding for the human-referee arm. **No error rates.**
- Andersen, L. E. (2020). "Acceptable gaps in mathematical proofs." *Synthese*. doi:10.1007/s11229-018-1778-8 (interviews with mathematicians on refereeing). Andersen 2017 and Mejia-Ramos & Weber 2014 (a survey of 54 mathematicians on refereeing) are cited within Greiffenhagen. **Retrieve directly.**
- Brent, R. P. "Some instructive mathematical errors." arXiv:2106.07269. A catalogue of long-undetected errors.
- Hales, T. "Mathematics in the Age of the Turing Machine." arXiv:1302.2898. Includes Boas's remark that proofs are wrong "half the time" (anecdotal).
- Yampolskiy, "Verifier Theory and Unverifiability." arXiv:1609.00331. On p. 2 it states "It has been estimated that at least a third of all mathematical publications contain errors" and attributes this to its reference [8]. **That confirms the figure is secondhand; the original source was not traced and should be before use.**
- **Cost datapoints:** Hard2Verify (arXiv:2510.13744) was "produced with over 500 hours of human labor" by PhD-level annotators for 200 responses, about 2.5 expert-hours per response (derived, not reported as such). The paper evaluates 29 generative critics and process reward models and finds that "beyond a few standouts, open-source verifiers lag closed source models". The ICCM pipeline (arXiv:2602.13695) generated solutions in minutes but needed hours to verify one First Proof problem. Open Proof Corpus estimates individual judge error rates from double-graded items.
- **Not found:** blinded planted-error studies with professional mathematicians, or any quantitative estimate of "verification debt". SYNDEOM's human arm with decoys appears **novel**, which also means it has **no external baseline**.

## Verdict

**(1) Does any single source falsify CLAIM-0006 as stated?** **No.** No source found states a detector's error rate jointly as a function of proof length *and* flaw class for AI-generated research-level proofs. The closest candidates each miss one or more conditions:
- Li et al. 2406.00755: type × length, but competition level and one class.
- ProofGrid 2605.12524: length curves, but propositional logic and no flaw classes.
- PRMBench: type × position, competition level.
- AdvancedMathBench and IMProofBench: type only, advanced or research level.
- Hard2Verify and ArxivMathGradingBench: research-level ground truth, but neither breakdown.

**(2) Narrowest surviving version.** *"As of 12 October 2026, no published work reports the detection performance (e.g., ROC/AUC or FPR/FNR) of any proof-triage method—human or automated—on research-level, AI-generated mathematical proofs broken down jointly by flaw class and by proof length. Prior work reports breakdowns by error type alone (PRMBench, AdvancedMathBench, IMProofBench), by length or step position alone (PRMBench, ProofGrid, Daheim et al.), or both only for a single error class on competition-level problems (Li et al. 2024); none defines a length at which a detector falls to chance."* SYNDEOM should additionally claim novelty for: (a) the statement-drift × valid-Lean-proof class, (b) axiom-smuggling detection measured as a calibrated detector rather than a filter, and (c) blinded human referees with decoys. Each of these should carry an "as far as this sweep found" hedge.

**(3) Ten sources SYNDEOM must cite, ranked:**
1. Li et al., arXiv:2406.00755. The only type × length result, so the claim must be scoped around it.
2. PRMBench, arXiv:2501.03124. Error-type taxonomy, position analysis and an injection precedent.
3. Hard2Verify, arXiv:2510.13744 / ACL 2026. Frontier step-level ground truth.
4. AdvancedMathBench, arXiv:2607.11849. Advanced-level verifier FP/FN by error category (cite the version).
5. ProofGrid, arXiv:2605.12524. Accuracy vs proof length precedent.
6. FormalRewardBench, arXiv:2605.10141. Lean injection including verbose/long incorrect proofs.
7. Pseudo-Formalization / ArxivMathGradingBench, arXiv:2605.20531. Real research-paper errors; external validation set.
8. "Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking," arXiv:2606.29493, together with leanprover/comparator. Statement fidelity and axiom hygiene.
9. AGMAI "Responsible Release," 29 Sept 2026 (S-0002), together with the register's S-0006 record of the 7 October withdrawals. The governing artifact standard and the first real-world labelled flaws.
10. ProcessBench, arXiv:2412.06559. Canonical first-error-step task definition.

Runners-up: IMProofBench (inter-rater agreement), Open Proof Corpus, FormalAlign / Beyond Compilation / ShadowBench, Sadasivan et al. (TV-bound template for fooling length), First Proof batches 1–2, Greiffenhagen 2024.
